  • Reassess at 48 hours
  • De-escalate when possible
  • Duration depends on site & severity

Ampicillin

Beta-lactam (Penicillin group) | IV/IM

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Community-acquired pneumonia (Neonates):
50 mg/kg/dose every 6–12 hours; refer to neonatal dosing section
Pyelonephritis/Urosepsis (>1 month):
50 mg/kg/dose IV every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)
Sepsis (Neonatal):
0–7 days: 50 mg/kg/dose every 12 hours
8–28 days: 50 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours
Intra-abdominal/GI infections (>1 month):
50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Respiratory — Birth to 1 week:
50 mg/kg/dose q12h (maximum 1.2 g/dose)
Septicemia (if no meningitis, HiB immune):
25 mg/kg/dose q6h (maximum 2g/dose)
Neonatal meningitis:
0–7 days: 50 mg/kg/dose q12h
8–28 days: 50 mg/kg/dose q8h

Adult Dosing

Standard IV/IM infections (pneumonia, UTI, soft tissue):
500 mg–1 g IV/IM every 6 hours (2–8 g/day total)
Severe/serious infections:
2 g IV every 4–6 hours (8–12 g/day)
Meningitis:
2 g IV every 4 hours (8–12 g/day total); some recommend up to 2 g q3h for optimal CNS penetration
Endocarditis (streptococcal):
2–3 g IV every 4 hours (12–18 g/day)
Renal dosing (CrCl <50 mL/min):
Reduce frequency; consult guidelines for specific adjustments
TDM: Not routinely required. Half-life: ~1 hour. Renal adjustment: Yes (moderate to significant). Note: Refer to neonatal dosing sections in source guidelines for exact neonatal regimens.

Amoxicillin

Beta-lactam (Penicillin group) | PO

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Community-acquired pneumonia (>1 month, uncomplicated):
30 mg/kg/dose orally every 8 hours (maximum 1 g/dose)
Uncomplicated UTI (>3 months):
Not primary first-line; prefer TMP-SMX or cefalexin
Otitis media:
30 mg/kg/dose orally every 8 hours (maximum 1 g/dose) for 5 days

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Respiratory — 1 week to 4 years:
Not detailed as IV; Benzylpenicillin IV preferred
Oral use:
Standard dosing: 30 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours

Adult Dosing

Mild-to-moderate infections (standard):
500 mg orally every 8 hours (1.5 g/day) OR 875 mg twice daily
Severe infections or pneumonia:
875 mg–1 g orally every 8 hours (2.6–3 g/day)
Acute otitis media (high-dose):
875 mg twice daily or 500 mg three times daily
Helicobacter pylori (part of triple therapy):
1 g orally twice daily for 10–14 days (with PPI + clarithromycin or PPI + metronidazole)
Gonorrhea (single dose):
3 g orally as a single dose (now less commonly used due to resistance)
Renal dosing (CrCl <30 mL/min):
500 mg orally every 12 hours
Route: Primarily oral; IV forms rarely used in adults. Bioavailability: ~80% orally (high, take with or without food). Half-life: ~1 hour. Note: Extended-release formulations (1 g once daily) available but less common in US.

Vancomycin

Glycopeptide | IV

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Meningitis (CSF penetration—Gram-positive cocci):
15 mg/kg/dose IV every 6 hours (maximum initial dose 750 mg). Perform TDM.
CNS/Meningitis (>1 month):
15 mg/kg/dose IV every 6 hours; discuss with ID
Community-acquired sepsis (if MRSA risk):
15 mg/kg every 6 hours (maximum initial 750 mg). Perform TDM.
Bone & joint (if mrMRSA):
15 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum initial dose 750 mg). Perform TDM.

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Meningitis (if G+ cocci in CSF):
15 mg/kg/dose q6h (maximum 500 mg/dose)
Hospital-acquired sepsis:
15 mg/kg/dose q8h

Adult Dosing

Standard infections (intermittent dosing):
15–20 mg/kg IV every 8–12 hours (typical: 1–1.5 g every 8–12 h); target trough 15–20 mcg/mL
Serious infections (SAE, endocarditis):
15–20 mg/kg IV every 6–8 hours; target trough 15–20 mcg/mL (some experts recommend 20–25 for MRSA)
Meningitis (CNS penetration critical):
15–20 mg/kg IV every 4–6 hours (typical: 2 g every 6–8 h OR 15–20 mg/kg q4–6h for optimal CSF levels); target trough 20–25 mcg/mL (or AUC-guided: AUC 400–600 mg·h/L)
Continuous IV infusion (alternative):
Loading dose 25–30 mg/kg, then 30–40 mg/kg/day as continuous infusion; monitor via AUC target (preferred)
Renal dosing (CrCl):
Normal renal function: standard dosing
CrCl 40–60: q12h
CrCl <40: reduce dose significantly (often q24–48h or longer); requires TDM guidance
TDM MANDATORY: Target trough 15–20 mcg/mL (standard) or 20–25 for serious infections/meningitis; AUC-guided dosing (target AUC 400–600 mg·h/L) now preferred. Draw trough just before 4th dose (steady-state). Half-life: 4–6 hours (normal renal function). CNS penetration: 15–30% (variable; higher with meningeal inflammation). Note: Reserve for MRSA/resistant organisms or penicillin allergy; preferred over linezolid for cost/safety.

Ceftriaxone

Beta-lactam (3rd-generation Cephalosporin) | IV/IM

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Community-acquired sepsis (non-PICU, >1 month):
100 mg/kg once daily (maximum 4 g/day); OR 50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours
Meningitis (>1 month):
100 mg/kg/dose (maximum 4 g/dose) once daily
Lower respiratory (CAP, HAP, aspiration):
50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)
Intra-abdominal/GI:
50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)
Bone & Joint (alternative dosing):
Referenced as alternative; check full guideline for site-specific dosing

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Meningitis (if G+ cocci in CSF):
50 mg/kg/dose q12h (maximum 2 g/dose)
Respiratory (severe, toxic ± O₂):
50 mg/kg/dose q24h (maximum 2 g/dose)
GI (peritonitis, cholangitis, NEC, appendicitis):
50 mg/kg/dose q12h (maximum 2 g/dose)

Adult Dosing

Standard infections (respiratory, soft tissue, intra-abdominal):
1 g IV/IM every 12 hours OR 500 mg every 8 hours (total 2–4 g/day)
Moderate-to-severe infections:
2 g IV every 12 hours (4 g/day total)
Meningitis (excellent CNS penetration):
2 g IV every 12 hours (4 g/day); some use 2 g q8h for optimal CSF levels in severe meningitis
Gonorrhea (single IM dose):
250 mg IM once (now standard for gonorrhea, often with azithromycin or doxycycline for chlamydia)
Surgical prophylaxis (single preop dose):
1–2 g IV once (redose every 12 hours if prolonged procedure)
Endocarditis (streptococcal, native valve):
2 g IV every 12 hours for 4 weeks
Renal dosing:
Minimal adjustment needed (hepatic metabolism). Use normal dosing even in CrCl <10 mL/min
Advantages: Excellent CNS/tissue penetration, long half-life (~8 hours), minimal renal adjustment needed, IV/IM option, excellent OPAT agent. Biliary excretion: ~40–60%; good for biliary infections. Note: Long half-life allows once- or twice-daily dosing, ideal for outpatient therapy.

Cefotaxime

Beta-lactam (3rd-generation Cephalosporin) | IV/IM

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Community-acquired sepsis (non-PICU, >1 month):
50 mg/kg/dose IV every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose); review at 48 hours
Meningitis (>1 month):
50 mg/kg/dose IV every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)
CNS/Meningitis (>1 month):
Cefotaxime IV 50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)
Intra-abdominal/GI (peritonitis):
50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Respiratory (severe, toxic ± O₂):
25 mg/kg/dose q8h (maximum 2 g/dose)
Neonatal meningitis:
Age-dependent; refer to neonatal dosing section

Adult Dosing

Standard infections (sepsis, pneumonia, UTI):
1–2 g IV/IM every 8 hours (3–6 g/day total)
Severe infections:
2 g IV every 6–8 hours (6–8 g/day)
Meningitis (preferred 3rd-gen ceph for CNS penetration):
2 g IV every 4 hours (8 g/day) OR 2 g every 4–6 hours depending on severity (some use up to 12 g/day)
Endocarditis or bacteremia:
2 g IV every 6–8 hours (8–12 g/day)
Gynecologic infections (intra-abdominal):
2 g IV every 6 hours
Renal dosing (CrCl):
CrCl 50–80: standard dosing
CrCl 10–50: reduce frequency (q8–12h depending on severity)
CrCl <10: q24h
Advantages: Superior CNS penetration vs. ceftriaxone (35–50% CSF penetration), excellent for meningitis, short half-life (~1 hour) requires frequent dosing. Renal adjustment: Moderate. Note: Often preferred over ceftriaxone for meningitis due to better CSF levels; hepatic metabolism (60%), renal (40%).

Clindamycin

Lincosamide | IV/PO | ⚠️ Limited CNS penetration

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Oral/Dental (allergy option for penicillin-allergic):
Clindamycin 10 mg/kg/dose orally three times daily (maximum 600 mg/dose)
Lower respiratory (PICU, if life-threatening or MRSA):
15 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours (maximum 1.2 g/dose)
Severe cellulitis (inpatient, if nmMRSA):
15 mg/kg/dose IV every 8 hours (maximum 1.2 g/dose)

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Oral/Dental:
Standard pediatric dosing per age; refer to full guideline
Oral aspiration (severe):
Clindamycin recommended for anaerobic coverage

Adult Dosing

Mild-to-moderate infections (PO):
300 mg orally every 6–8 hours (1.2–1.8 g/day)
Severe infections (PO):
450 mg orally every 6 hours (1.8 g/day)
IV/IM (serious infections):
600–900 mg IV/IM every 6–8 hours (2.4–3.6 g/day); up to 1.2 g q4h for life-threatening
Bone & joint (severe MRSA):
900 mg IV every 8 hours (2.7 g/day)
Aspiration pneumonia (anaerobic coverage):
600 mg IV every 6–8 hours (2.4 g/day)
Gynecologic infections:
600 mg IV every 6–8 hours (2.4 g/day)
Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID):
900 mg IV every 8 hours for 24 hours after clinical improvement, then switch to PO (450 mg q6h)
C. difficile colitis (pseudomembranous colitis):
300 mg orally four times daily for 7–10 days (now less preferred; vancomycin or fidaxomicin preferred)
Renal dosing:
No dose adjustment needed; hepatic metabolism
⚠️ CRITICAL — Does NOT provide CNS penetration. Do NOT use as monotherapy for meningitis. Advantages: Good anaerobic coverage (Bacteroides, Peptostreptococcus, Clostridium, Prevotella), MRSA coverage, excellent oral bioavailability, IV/PO option, good skin/soft tissue penetration. Caution: C. difficile-associated diarrhea (dose/duration dependent; limit to <7–10 days); allergic cross-reactivity with erythromycin (5–10%); hepatotoxicity (rare).

Linezolid

Oxazolidinone | IV/PO

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

MRSA coverage (alternative to vancomycin):
Referenced in bone/joint and other MRSA-resistant infections; exact pediatric dosing not detailed in snippet

General Pediatric Dosing

Infants & children:
10 mg/kg IV or PO every 8 hours (younger children may require higher mg/kg doses)
Older children/adolescents:
600 mg IV or PO every 12 hours

Adult Dosing

Standard infections (skin, soft tissue, pneumonia, VRE):
600 mg IV or PO every 12 hours (1.2 g/day)
Bone & joint infections (MRSA):
600 mg IV or PO every 8 hours (1.8 g/day)
Meningitis or CNS infections:
600 mg IV every 8 hours (1.8 g/day); good CSF penetration (60–90%)
Nosocomial pneumonia (resistant Gram-negatives or MRSA):
600 mg IV every 12 hours (1.2 g/day)
Bacteremia or endocarditis (MRSA/VRE):
600 mg every 8 hours; some recommend 600 mg q6h for optimal levels
Renal dosing:
No adjustment needed; <5% renal excretion
Hepatic impairment:
Use with caution; no formal dosing adjustments, but monitor closely
Advantages: Excellent CNS penetration (60–90% CSF levels), 100% oral bioavailability (no IV necessary if tolerated PO), MRSA/VRE coverage, no TDM required, rapidly bactericidal. Caution: Monitor CBC baseline and weekly (thrombocytopenia, anemia with prolonged use); serotonin syndrome with SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs; lactic acidosis (rare, chronic use); B12 deficiency (rare). Cost: Expensive (often $100+ per dose IV). Duration: Limit to 28 days when possible.

Gentamicin

Aminoglycoside | IV/IM

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Endocarditis (>1 month, <10 years):
7.5 mg/kg once daily (maximum 320 mg/day). Perform TDM.
Endocarditis (>10 years):
6 mg/kg once daily (maximum 560 mg/day). Perform TDM.
Bone & Joint (>1 month to <10 years):
7.5 mg/kg once daily (maximum 320 mg/day)
Bone & Joint (>10 years):
6 mg/kg once daily (maximum 560 mg/day)
Note (all): Perform TDM; adjust for renal function

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Neonatal (<2 kg, <1 month old):
2.5 mg/kg/dose q24h
Normal gestation (2–4 weeks):
5 mg/kg/dose q24h
Premature, <30 weeks:
2.5 mg/kg/dose q36h

Adult Dosing

Once-daily dosing (preferred for most infections):
5–7 mg/kg IV/IM once daily (typical: 400–600 mg once daily for 70 kg adult); target peak 20–30 mcg/mL, trough <1 mcg/mL
Traditional divided dosing (for synergy in endocarditis):
1–1.5 mg/kg IV/IM every 8 hours (3–4.5 mg/kg/day total); target peak 5–10 mcg/mL, trough 0.5–2 mcg/mL
Severe sepsis/endocarditis (empiric while awaiting TDM):
Loading: 2 mg/kg IV then 1.7 mg/kg every 8 hours (or 7 mg/kg once daily)
Renal dosing (CrCl):
Normal (>80): standard dosing
CrCl 40–80: 5–7 mg/kg every 24h or 1–1.5 mg/kg every 12h
CrCl 10–40: 2–3.5 mg/kg every 24h
CrCl <10: reduced dosing, TDM guided (often q48–72h)
Obesity (dose based on ideal body weight):
Use IBW + 0.4(actual - IBW) for dosing
TDM MANDATORY: Once-daily regimen: check peak (1 h post-dose, target 20–30) and trough (pre-dose, target <1); traditional dosing: peak (0.5–1 h post-dose, 5–10) and trough (pre-dose, 0.5–2). Draw at steady-state (after 3rd–4th dose). Half-life: 2–3 hours (normal renal function). Renal adjustment: Significant. Note: Once-daily preferred; lower toxicity, better efficacy vs. divided dosing.

Metronidazole

Nitroimidazole (Anaerobic) | IV/PO

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Intra-abdominal/GI (peritonitis, appendicitis):
7.5 mg/kg/dose IV every 8 hours (maximum 500 mg/dose)
Neonatal (metronidazole):
Body Wt <2000g: 7.5 mg/kg/dose q24h (0–7 days), q12h (8–28 days)
Body Wt >2000g: 7.5 mg/kg/dose q12h (0–28 days)

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Peritonitis/GI/Appendicitis:
12.5 mg/kg/dose q12h (e.g., 250 mg q12h for adolescents)
Neonatal:
Age- and weight-dependent; refer to neonatal section

Adult Dosing

Anaerobic intra-abdominal infections:
500 mg IV every 8 hours (1.5 g/day); OR 500 mg PO every 6–8 hours (2–4 g/day)
Severe anaerobic sepsis:
Loading dose: 15 mg/kg IV, then 7.5 mg/kg IV every 6–8 hours
Pseudomembranous colitis (C. difficile):
500 mg orally three times daily for 10–14 days (now often replaced by fidaxomicin for fulminant)
Giardiasis:
250 mg orally three times daily for 5–7 days (or 2 g once daily for 3 days)
Trichomoniasis (vaginitis):
2 g orally as single dose OR 500 mg twice daily for 7 days
Amebiasis (intestinal):
750 mg orally three times daily for 5–10 days (followed by luminal agent like paromomycin)
Helminths/Giardia (alternative):
400 mg every 8 hours for 5–7 days
Renal dosing (CrCl):
Minimal adjustment; can use standard dosing even with severe renal disease
Advantages: Excellent anaerobic coverage (Bacteroides, Peptostreptococcus, Clostridium, Prevotella), good CNS penetration (60–80% CSF levels), IV/PO option, inexpensive. Caution: Metallic taste, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol (avoid), peripheral neuropathy/CNS toxicity with prolonged use (>3 weeks), seizures (rare, high doses/renal failure), interaction with warfarin. Adverse effects: GI upset, dark/brown urine (harmless). Half-life: 6–8 hours.

Ciprofloxacin

Fluoroquinolone | IV/PO

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Meningitis (immediate-type hypersensitivity alternative):
Ciprofloxacin IV 10 mg/kg/dose 8-hourly (maximum 400 mg/dose)
Prophylaxis for N. meningitidis (alternative to Rifampicin):
3 months–5 years: 30 mg/kg (up to 125 mg) orally as a single dose
5–12 years: 250 mg orally as a single dose
>12 years: 500 mg orally as a single dose

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Orthopedic (Gram-negative osteo):
Ciprofloxacin recommended for selected cases

Adult Dosing

Uncomplicated UTI:
250 mg orally twice daily for 3 days
Uncomplicated cystitis (acute):
500 mg orally twice daily for 3 days
Complicated UTI/pyelonephritis:
500 mg orally twice daily for 7–14 days (or 400 mg IV every 12 hours)
Respiratory/lower respiratory infection:
500–750 mg orally twice daily for 7–14 days
Bone & joint (osteomyelitis):
750 mg orally twice daily for 4–6 weeks
Gram-negative sepsis/serious infection:
400 mg IV every 8–12 hours (1.2 g/day)
Meningitis prophylaxis (N. meningitidis):
500 mg orally as single dose (or 250 mg IM if available)
Anthrax prophylaxis (post-exposure):
500 mg orally twice daily for 60 days
Renal dosing (CrCl):
CrCl >50: standard dosing
CrCl 30–50: reduce to 500 mg q12h (not 750 mg)
CrCl <30: 250–500 mg q12h
Advantages: Good Gram-negative coverage (including Pseudomonas), fluoroquinolone class, excellent oral bioavailability (70%), IV/PO option, good urinary penetration. Caution: QT prolongation risk, CNS effects (tremor, confusion, seizures—rare), tendinopathy (especially with age >60, corticosteroid use), C. difficile risk, photosensitivity (rare). Note: Resistance to Staph/Strep common; moderate anaerobic coverage.

Azithromycin

Macrolide | IV/PO

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Pertussis:
≤6 months: 10 mg/kg orally once daily (maximum 500 mg/day) for 5 days
>6 months: 10 mg/kg on Day 1 (maximum 500 mg), then 5 mg/kg daily on Days 2–5 (maximum 250 mg/day)
Lower respiratory (PICU, if M. pneumoniae suspected):
Azithromycin IV 10 mg/kg once daily (maximum 500 mg/day). Switch to oral azithromycin after 24 hours.

Pediatric Dosing (RCH 2005)

Respiratory (mycoplasma):
4 mg/kg/dose q12h (RCH notes roxithromycin for mycoplasma)

Adult Dosing

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP):
500 mg orally on Day 1, then 250 mg daily for 4 more days (Z-pack regimen, total 1.5 g)
Respiratory infection (alternative course):
500 mg once daily for 3 days (total 1.5 g)
Atypical pathogens (Chlamydia/Mycoplasma/Legionella):
500 mg on Day 1, then 250 mg daily for 4–6 days
Acute otitis media or sinusitis:
500 mg once daily for 3 days
Pertussis (whooping cough):
500 mg on Day 1, then 250 mg daily for 4 days
Gonorrhea (single dose):
2 g orally as single dose (now less common; used with ceftriaxone)
Helicobacter pylori eradication (triple therapy):
500 mg orally daily with PPI + amoxicillin for 10–14 days
MAC prophylaxis (CD4 <50 in HIV):
1.2 g once weekly (or 600 mg twice weekly)
Renal dosing:
Minimal adjustment needed; mainly hepatic metabolism
Advantages: Excellent atypical coverage (Chlamydia, Mycoplasma, Legionella), long half-life (~68 hours allowing once-daily dosing), good lung/tissue penetration, oral option, inexpensive. Caution: QT prolongation risk (especially with other QT-prolonging drugs, electrolyte abnormalities, or old age); C. difficile risk; interactions with many drugs (CYP3A4 inhibitor); caution in cardiac patients. Adverse effects: GI upset, taste disturbance, hepatotoxicity (rare).

Cefazolin

Beta-lactam (1st-generation Cephalosporin) | IV/IM

Pediatric Dosing (CHQ v12.0)

Osteomyelitis/Septic Arthritis (<5 years):
50 mg/kg/dose (maximum 2 g/dose) every 8 hours
Periorbital/Orbital Cellulitis (<5 years, not HiB-vaccinated):
50 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours (maximum 2 g/dose)
